# Initial Setup
In my first attempt to perform this experiment I was using a lightweight driver and a plastic cap, with the same nylon screw, plate, and enclosure. However, I noticed that with the full setup in place, there was some noticeable compression from the driver due to the weight of the plate. I also only leveled the plate once it was on the stool, but I failed to account for the fact that the floor wasn't perfectly level, or the stool, so when I got it level the plate was still pretty uneven relative to the enclosure. I tried finding the resonances from using a phone app off to the side rather than a centered microphone over the plate. I would look at the total SPL at a frequency, not the strength of the particular frequency I was using. So when I performed my full sweep of the frequency range I made sure I hit the frequencies where I got the loudest response measured at that position. It's also worth noting that while I intended to sweep through the entire 50-1000 Hz range, I found it difficult to get much of a response out of this driver at frequencies above 700 Hz. When I tried going above that, I had to turn up the amplifier considerably, but this caused the wires of the driver to overheat and almost fry the driver, so I stayed away from the 700-1000 Hz region.

# Improved Setup
In my 2nd and final attempt to perform this experiment, I made a lot of changes. I used a much stronger driver which wouldn't get compressed by the plate, and wouldn't get its wires burnt when I turned up the amplifier. Rather than using a plastic cap, I used a hard rubber ring, with a fitted cap attached to hold the nylon screw with, which was placed into the plate in the same way as before. However, this time I made sure that the plate was level before setting it into the enclosure. So now I had a properly aligned plate, and a driver which would allow me to generate stronger vibrations in the plate. I also set up a microphone to detect the SPdBV at particular frequencies rather than using a phone application to detect the SPL at all frequencies. I used some digital software alongside this microphone that allowed me to sweep through the desired range immediately and see where I get the strongest SPdBV response. I was able to sweep through the 50-750 Hz region. However, I found that going above that required me to produce very loud sounds, which I could do, but due to environmental limitations I wanted to avoid. Though all of the data in the previous region I still took at a considerably louder volume in general. Rather than just sweeping through each frequency in 5 Hz increments, I first did a quick sweep through the 50-1000 Hz range and looked at the resulting frequency vs SPdBV curve to determine where the loudest responses were in the range. I figured that these locations would give me the most distinct shapes so I investigated these frequencies first. Then I checked where my simulations predicted resonance, where my previous experiment predicted them to be, I tried to see if I found a harmonic, and then finally I investigated any regions I missed in 5 Hz increments to see if I missed any notable patterns.

# Results
Now that I had a more effective method to predict where I would get a strong visual and auditory response, my results generally had more distinct shapes, and the sugar more effectively moved into nodal lines. I was also able to use a stronger input now that I wasn't limited by the issues with the weaker driver, which further improved these outcomes.

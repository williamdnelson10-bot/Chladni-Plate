# Initial Setup
In our first attempt to perform this experiment we were using a lightweight driver and a plastic cap, with the same nylon screw, plate, and enclosure. However, we noticed that with the full setup in place, there was some noticeable compression from the driver due to the weight of the plate. We also only leveled the plate once it was on the stool, but we failed to account for the fact that the floor wasn't perfectly level, or the stool, so when we got it level the plate was still pretty uneven relative to the enclosure. We tried finding the resonances from using a phone app off to the side rather than a centered microphone over the plate. We would look at the total SPL at a frequency, not the strength of the particular frequency we were using. So when we performed our full sweep of the frequency range we made sure we hit the frequencies where we got the loudest response measured at that position. It's also worth noting that while we intended to sweep through the entire 50-1000 Hz range, we found it difficult to get much of a response out of this driver at frequencies above 700 Hz. When we tried going above that, we had to turn up the amplifier considerably, but this caused the wires of the driver to overheat and almost fry the driver, so we stayed away from the 700-1000 Hz region.

# Improved Setup
In our 2nd and final attempt to perform this experiment, we made a lot of changes. We used a much stronger driver which wouldn't get compressed by the plate, and wouldn't get its wires burnt when we turned up the amplifier. Rather than using a plastic cap, we used a hard rubber ring, with a fitted cap attached to hold the nylon screw with, which was placed into the plate in the same way as before. However, this time we made sure that the plate was level before setting it into the enclosure. So now we had a properly aligned plate, and a driver which would allow us to generate stronger vibrations in the plate. We also set up a microphone to detect the SPdBV at particular frequencies rather than using a phone application to detect the SPL at all frequencies. We used some digital software alongside this microphone that allowed us to sweep through the desired range immediately and see where we get the strongest SPdBV response. We were able to sweep through the 50-750 Hz region, however we found that going above that required us to produce very loud sounds, which we could do, but due to environmental limitations we wanted to avoid. Though all of the data in the previous region we still took at a considerably louder volume in general. Rather than just sweeping through each frequency in 5 Hz increments, we first did a quick sweep through the 50-1000 Hz range and looked at the resulting frequency vs SPdBV curve to determine where the loudest responses were in the range. We figured that these locations would give us the most distinct shapes so we investigated these frequencies first. Then we checked where our simulations predicted resonance, where our previous experiment predicted them to be, we tried to see if we found a harmonic, and then finally we investigated any regions we missed in 5 Hz increments to see if we missed any notable patterns.

# Results
Now that we had a more effective method to predict where we would get a strong visual and auditory response, our results generally had more distinct shapes, and the sugar more effectively moved into nodal lines. We were also able to use a stronger input now that we weren't limited by the issues with the weaker driver, which further improved these outcomes.
